Hardware Calibration Guide

Recommended Thermal Printer Settings for Flipkart Sellers

To prevent barcode scanning failures during Ekart Logistics pickups and ensure crisp text on 203 DPI & 300 DPI thermal heads, configure your driver as follows:

Paper / Media Stock Size Set Page Size to 4.00 × 6.00 inches (or 100 × 150 mm). Never select Letter or A4 in printer preferences.
Print Scaling / Size Select Actual Size (100%). Disable "Fit to Printable Area" or "Shrink Oversized Pages" to maintain barcode bar ratios.
Darkness / Density Set darkness to 12 - 14 (on TSC TE244 / Zebra ZD230) to ensure deep black tracking bars without ink bleeding.
Print Speed Set print speed to 3 or 4 inches per second (ips) for the sharpest edge definition on 1D barcodes and QR codes.

The Problem with Direct A4 Printing on 4×6" Thermal Barcode Printers

Direct thermal printers such as the TSC TE244, Zebra ZD230, TVS LP 46 Neo, and Xprinter XP-420B are engineered to accept 4×6 inch (100×150 mm) roll stickers. When you send an uncropped A4 PDF directly to these printers, the print driver attempts to scale down the entire A4 sheet. This results in severe operational bottlenecks:

  • Ekart Barcode Scan Failure: Scaling down a full A4 sheet shrinks the 1D Code 128 tracking barcode below the optical scanner threshold, causing pickup associates from Ekart Logistics, Delhivery, and Shadowfax to reject parcels at handover.
  • Thermal Sticker Waste: Customer tax invoices are printed on expensive direct thermal sticker paper rather than standard laser paper, increasing your packaging supply costs.

Understanding FSN vs. SKU on Flipkart Labels

Flipkart's own internal product identifier is the FSN (Flipkart Serial Number) — a code Flipkart assigns to every unique listing on its catalog, distinct from whatever SKU a seller uses internally for their own inventory tracking. Both codes commonly appear on a Flipkart shipping label, and this tool's parser checks for either one: if a page's text contains an explicit "SKU:" marker it uses that, and falls back to reading the "FSN:" marker when SKU isn't present. This dual-detection matters because different sellers configure their listings differently — some rely entirely on Flipkart's FSN as their sorting key, others maintain their own SKU scheme and only reference FSN incidentally — and grouping needs to work correctly either way for sorting to actually cluster identical products together.

How Text-Marker Boundary Detection Works for Flipkart Labels

A Flipkart order PDF is, like any PDF, just positioned text and vector graphics — nothing in the file format itself marks "this is the label" versus "this is the invoice." This tool finds that boundary by scanning each page for text markers Flipkart commonly prints near the invoice section: phrases like "Tax Invoice," "Retail Invoice," "Invoice Number," "Ordered through Flipkart," and "Sold By." Whichever marker appears first establishes the vertical cut point between the shipping label above and the invoice below, shown live in the interactive preview so it can be checked before committing to a full batch.

Choosing Between Thermal 4×6", A4 4-in-1, and A4 2-in-1

The right layout depends on what printer is sitting at the packing station. A dedicated thermal roll printer should always use Thermal 4×6" — one label per output page at the printer's native size, with no ink cost since direct-thermal printing uses heat rather than ink or toner. Sellers without a thermal printer get two A4 options instead: A4 4-in-1 tiles four labels per sheet in a 2×2 grid for maximum paper efficiency, at the cost of needing scissors or a cutter afterward; A4 2-in-1 stacks two labels per sheet, giving each one more room and requiring only a single straight cut, at roughly double the per-label paper cost compared to the 4-in-1 grid. Neither A4 mode is objectively better — it comes down to cutting tolerance and monthly sticker-sheet budget, and both are one click apart from the same layout selector.

Common Causes of Barcode Rejection at Ekart Pickup

  • Undersized barcode from uncropped printing — by far the most common cause, and the one this tool directly eliminates by isolating the label at true native size before it ever reaches the printer.
  • Incorrect print scale — even a correctly cropped label scans poorly if the printer driver is set to "Fit to Printable Area" instead of Actual Size / 100%; this setting is worth re-checking after any driver update.
  • Thermal head wear — direct-thermal print quality degrades gradually as a print head ages, so a darkness setting that produced crisp barcodes months ago may need adjusting upward over time.
  • Physical label damage — creases across a barcode from applying it to a curved or textured surface can distort bar widths enough to defeat a handheld scanner even on an otherwise perfect print.

Understanding the Multi-Quantity Badge

Under-shipment — a buyer ordering two or three units but receiving only one — is a specific, recurring packing error that has little to do with product quality and everything to do with a packer glancing quickly at a label and missing a small quantity indicator among other order details. It tends to happen most on busy dispatch days, when many visually similar labels move through a packing station quickly. The Multi-Quantity Warning Badge interrupts exactly that failure mode: any order whose parsed quantity is two or higher gets a bold red "QTY: X (MULTI)" badge stamped directly on the visible label, placed prominently enough that a packer notices it before sealing the parcel rather than after a customer complaint arrives.

Paper and Cost Considerations Across Layout Modes

For sellers weighing thermal rolls against A4 sticker sheets purely on cost, thermal roll stock costs a modest amount per label but requires owning a thermal printer upfront, with no ongoing ink or toner cost since direct-thermal printing uses heat rather than ink. A4 sticker sheets work with a conventional laser or inkjet printer most sellers already own, but cost more per sheet and add an ongoing ink or toner cost on top. Between the two A4 modes, 4-in-1 stretches a sticker sheet further by fitting four labels instead of two — a difference that matters more as monthly order volume climbs.

Frequently Overlooked Printer Driver Settings

Beyond paper size, scale, darkness, and speed, a few printer driver settings quietly cause scanning problems even on a correctly cropped label. Print orientation should stay Portrait for both thermal and A4 output — some drivers silently default to Landscape after an OS update. Color mode should be forced to monochrome on thermal printers specifically, since some drivers apply unnecessary color-processing overhead that slows printing without any visual benefit on a black-and-white thermal head. Margins should be set to zero or "None" wherever the driver allows, since a printer that insists on a default margin can clip the very edge of a label cropped to exactly fill the page.

Keeping Barcodes Scan-Ready Through the Whole Chain

A barcode that scans perfectly at the packing station but fails at Ekart pickup or last-mile delivery usually degraded somewhere between those two points, not at the moment of printing. Two habits help close that gap: store printed thermal rolls away from direct sunlight and heat, since direct-thermal paper is heat-sensitive and prolonged exposure can fade a print over hours even without anyone touching it; and apply labels to a flat section of packaging wherever possible, since a barcode stretched across a rounded edge or crease distorts bar widths just enough to occasionally defeat a scanner even on a perfectly sharp print.

Environmental and Cost Impact of Cropped vs. Uncropped Printing

Beyond the barcode-scanning argument, there's a straightforward material-waste case for cropping before printing. Cropping before printing means every printed square inch of thermal roll or sticker sheet corresponds to something actually used, which is both the cheaper outcome and the lower-waste one.
